Everything your files need, in one place

A sync tool, a backup tool, a file manager, a duplicate finder and a bulk renamer usually are five programs. Here they share one window and one set of safety rules.

Synchronize with a master copy

A folder on your external disk is the master; each computer has its own folder that belongs to it. Both ways, master → computer, or computer → master. The disk carries the changes from one computer to the other.

Compare first, then run

Every run starts with a list of what would be copied and deleted, file by file. Nothing changes before you start it. Files changed on both sides are yours to decide — or the master wins, or the newer file.

A master it recognizes

The master is known by a marker inside it, not by its drive letter. Plugged in as F: instead of E:, it's found again; another disk at the old letter is refused.

Backup in plain files

An exact copy of a folder on another disk, which you can open with Explorer. Check for damage finds files whose content changed although their size and time didn't — what a failing disk does silently.

Two folders side by side

Copy, move, rename and delete between two panes, with breadcrumbs, sorting and the keys you know (F2, F5, F6, Delete). Send a folder straight to the duplicate finder or a selection to the renamer.

Merge folder trees

Bring several folder trees together in one. Files with the same content are taken once; for different files with the same path you choose: keep both, the newer, the target's, or replace.

Find duplicates

Files with exactly the same content, whatever they're called. Mark all but the oldest, the newest or the one on the master in a click. The last copy of a file always stays.

Find similar names

Report.docx, Report (1).docx, report_final_v2.docx: copies and versions that aren't identical are grouped by name. Numbered series like IMG_0012, IMG_0013 aren't mistaken for versions.

Rename in bulk

Replace text (also regular expressions), insert, remove, change case, number, add the file's date. Every new name is shown first, with what stands in its way — and the last rename can be undone.

Safe by construction

A sync tool that loses a file once is never trusted again. These rules hold for every operation.

  • Nothing is lost on purpose. What a synchronization, backup, merge or deletion replaces or removes goes to the folder's safety copies, and the app puts it back with a click.
  • Copies are verified. A copy is written beside its target, read back and compared with the original, and only then takes its place. An interrupted copy leaves the target as it was.
  • A missing disk isn't an empty folder. A folder that can't be read is an error, not a reason to delete its counterpart. A run that would delete most of a side asks again.
  • What changed meanwhile is skipped. A file that changed between the comparison and the run is left alone, not overwritten.
  • Nothing phones home. No cloud, no account, no telemetry, no update checks — and no analytics on this page.
  • Open source. Every line is on GitHub, under MIT or Apache-2.0.

Questions

Is Gotthard free?

Yes, for personal and commercial use. It's open source under the MIT or Apache-2.0 license.

How do I start?

Create a folder on your external disk, e.g. E:\Master\Photos (it may already hold your files). In Gotthard, add it under Synchronize, click Make it the master and choose this computer's folder. Compare, look at the list, Synchronize. On your other computer, add the same master folder — it's recognized — and that computer's folder.

Where do deleted files go?

Into the folder's safety copies: a hidden folder .gotthard\versions with one folder per run, holding the files as they were. The Safety copies view restores them and cleans up old ones. If you'd rather use the Recycle Bin, or delete for good, that's a setting.

Does it sync on its own?

Not yet. A synchronization or a backup runs when you start it, and always shows what it would do first. Runs on a schedule and when the disk is plugged in are planned.

Can it sync two computers over the network?

No: the external disk carries the changes. That's the point — your files never leave your own disks, and the master is a complete copy you can take with you.

Does Gotthard send my data anywhere?

No. There's no server, no account and no telemetry. Gotthard only reads and writes the folders you give it.

Is the master encrypted?

Gotthard keeps plain files, so that you can always get at them without it. To protect the disk, turn on BitLocker for it in Windows.
